# ELD PD Game: Room: How Counselors Support Our MLLs in Middle School

**Topic:** The school counselor's role in the academic, college/career, and social-emotional success of Multilingual Learners in grades 6–8
**Suggested campus level:** Middle school room (7th/8th). This topic looks at the whole system. It connects to:
- **Grading for Equity:** ELPAC and reclassification
- **MLLs with IEPs:** the student study team (SST) and difference vs. disability
- **Co-Teaching:** scheduling and collaboration
**Audience note:** written for counselors **and** teachers and administrators, because supporting MLLs is a shared job.
**Main California source:** Laurie Olsen, *A High School Counselor's Toolkit for English Learner Success: Implementing the English Learner Roadmap* (Californians Together, 2025). It's written for high school, but most of it applies to middle school, and it has a full section on middle-to-high-school alignment.
**Sections:** Part A = why counselors matter for MLLs · Part B = know your MLLs (typologies and life issues) · Part C = academic counseling (placement, scheduling, reclassification) · Part D = social-emotional support · Part E = family partnership · Part F = college/career and transitions · Part G = California laws and standards · Part H = game room blueprint

## PART A: Why Counselors Matter for MLLs

### A1. The Counselor's Three Domains (ASCA National Model)
| Domain | For MLLs, this means… |
|---|---|
| **Academic** | Correct placement, access to the full curriculum and electives, monitoring ELD progress and reclassification, preventing LTEL status |
| **College & Career** | Early, multilingual college/career awareness; A–G and biliteracy pathways starting in middle school; high school transition planning |
| **Social/Emotional** | Culture shock, acculturative stress, trauma, immigration-related anxiety, identity, belonging, peer relationships |

### A2. The Evidence
- **Counselors and course access:** in Oregon, EL-classified middle and high school students in schools **with any counselor time** were **more likely to be enrolled in core ELA and math courses** than ELs in schools with **no counselor** (ELRD Center / IES, 2023). *(Caution: schools with counselors were also larger and may simply offer more courses.)*
- **Counselor preparation matters:** McCall-Perez (2000) found that better-prepared counselors were linked to stronger achievement, graduation, social integration, and post-secondary planning for adolescent ELs (as summarized by the Oregon Dept. of Education).
- **What effective counseling programs do for ELs** (Johnson et al., 2016, via ODE): **collaborate with teachers**, refer to **mental health services**, provide **individual and group counseling**, organize **tutoring**, and **secure interpreters/translators**.
- **Culturally sustaining SEL:** bringing students' **worldviews, experiences, values, and cultural assets** into SEL improves ELs' social-emotional well-being (Lau & Shea, 2022).
- **Barriers ELs face** (Pagan-Rivera, 2014): learning a new language, forming peer relationships, low self-esteem, family separation during migration, adjusting to a new culture, navigating immigration law, and discrimination.

### A3. The Capacity Challenge
| | Students per counselor |
|---|---|
| **ASCA recommendation** | **250 : 1** |
| **U.S. average (2024–25)** | 372 : 1 |
| **California (2024–25)** | **≈ 432 : 1** (improved from 464:1 in 2022–23) |

> With caseloads this high, counselors **can't do it alone**. **Systems** (data dashboards, scheduling principles, teacher referral routines, family liaisons) multiply their impact.

## PART B: Know Your MLLs

### B1. English Learner Typologies (Californians Together Toolkit)
"English learner" is **not one group**. Counselors should build an **EL typology profile** of their school.

| Typology | Who they are | Counselor priorities |
|---|---|---|
| **Newcomers** (0–3 years in U.S. schools) | Recently arrived; vary widely in prior schooling | Orientation to U.S. school norms, transcript review, placement, SEL check-ins, family welcome |
| ↳ **Strong prior schooling** | Grade-level literacy and academics in the home language | **Accelerate**: place in rigorous courses with L1 resources; don't hold them back because of English |
| ↳ **Students with Interrupted Formal Education (SIFE)** | Gaps in schooling; may have limited literacy in L1 | Intensive literacy and numeracy support, tutoring, careful pacing, strong relationships |
| **Developing ELs** (4–6 years) | Progressing; "at risk" of becoming LTELs | Monitor ELPAC growth; prevent stalling |
| **Long-Term English Learners (LTELs)** (6+ years) | Often U.S.-born; socially fluent; stuck in academic English; may be disengaged | Goal-setting conferences about **reclassification**, motivation, appropriate ELD (separate from newcomers), access to electives and rigor |
| **Dually identified** (EL + IEP) | See the MLLs with IEPs room | Coordinate ELD + IEP services; SST "difference vs. disability" process |
| **Reclassified (RFEP)** | Recently exited EL status | **Monitor for 4 years** (state requirement); watch for slipping grades |

### B2. Life Issues That Affect Schooling (Californians Together Toolkit)
- **Culture shock:** new school norms (informal teacher relationships, voicing opinions, being corrected publicly, testing, changing classes at the bell) must be learned **before** academic learning can take off. Students may feel angry, depressed, or helpless.
- **War trauma / refugee experiences:** witnessing violence, family loss, years in refugee camps; possible PTSD, depression, trouble concentrating.
- **Unaccompanied minors:** may live with non-family adults and work to pay rent; spotty attendance; little adult guidance.
- **Arriving as an adolescent:** about **one-fifth** of CA ELs arrive as adolescents. That means a leap between school systems, with wide variation in prior schooling.
- **Immigration status and mixed-status families:** almost **half of CA schoolchildren have at least one immigrant parent**, and **1 in 5 CA children live in mixed-status households**. Fear of immigration enforcement can affect attendance, focus, and family engagement.
- **Family responsibilities:** interpreting for parents, caring for siblings, working.

### B3. The Four Core Stressors (U.S. Dept. of Education *Newcomer Toolkit*, Ch. 3)
The Newcomer Toolkit frames the stressors many immigrant and refugee students face, including:
- **Traumatic stress:** intense events that threaten or cause harm
- **Acculturative stress:** navigating between the new culture and the culture of origin, including language, discrimination, stigma, and bias
- **Resettlement stress:** housing, finances, legal status, access to services
- **Isolation:** loss of social support, friends, and community
**Protective factors:** emotion regulation, social support, parenting capacity, a welcoming school, and **trusted adults**.

## PART C: Academic Counseling, Placement, and Scheduling

### C1. Legal Baseline: Access to the Full Curriculum
Per the Californians Together Toolkit (summarizing state and federal requirements), ELs in middle and high school **cannot be denied enrollment** in:
- **Core curriculum courses** (ELA, math, science, history/social science)
- **Courses required for graduation**
- A **full course load**, including courses that meet **college-admission (A–G) requirements** and **advanced courses** (honors, accelerated)

### C2. Beware the "ESL Ghetto"
Building the master schedule around ELD can **lock ELs into the same group all day**. That limits English development, creates **social segregation**, and lowers expectations.
> *"Every effort must be made to avoid blocks of English learners spending all day across the curriculum with just other English learners."* (Californians Together, 2025)

### C3. Master Scheduling Principles (Oakland USD ELLMA, cited in the Toolkit)
| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Schedule ELD first** | Build ELD sections first, then the rest of the schedule around them |
| **Intentional grouping** | Group designated ELD by **typology** (newcomer, 4–6 year, LTEL). **Don't combine newcomers and LTELs**; their needs are very different |
| **Promote integration** | Minimize EL-only grouping **beyond** the designated ELD class |
| **Preserve electives** | Elective access for ELs is a **priority**. Electives (music, art, robotics, sports) drive engagement and belonging |
| **Content connection** | Connect designated ELD to content (e.g., the same teacher or texts as ELA) |
| **Acceleration, not remediation** | *"Time is of the essence"*: avoid wasting time; use L1 strengths, expanded learning time, and integrated ELD |

**Middle school solutions when the schedule is tight:**
- An **extra period / zero period** or an **advisory** used for ELD or support
- **Summer and Saturday intensives** (especially in middle school, to free up high school time)
- **Spanish for Native Speakers / heritage language** courses that build biliteracy and reach high school world-language readiness
- **Push-in co-teaching** (Co-Teaching room) to deliver integrated ELD in content classes

### C4. Placement Guidance by Typology (adapted from the Toolkit)
| Student | Placement guidance |
|---|---|
| Newcomer with **strong prior schooling** | Review the **foreign transcript** and/or test in L1; place in grade-level or **advanced** courses with L1 resources; intensive ELD with **flexibility to accelerate** |
| Newcomer **at grade level** | Newcomer support / orientation class (a few weeks to a semester); integrated ELD in content; tutoring |
| **SIFE** newcomer | Newcomer class; **double ELD** can help in year one; literacy/numeracy support; strong integrated ELD |
| **LTEL** | **LTEL-specific** designated ELD (academic language, writing, reading); access to rigor and electives; **reclassification goal-setting** |

### C5. ELPAC and Reclassification Monitoring
- **Know the data:** counselors should understand ELPAC levels, the CA ELD Standards, and reclassification (the Toolkit includes "A Counselor's Primer").
- **AB 2555 (CDE guidance):** the Summative ELPAC (**Overall PL 4**) is now the **sole reclassification criterion**. The CDE lists an effective date of **Sept 27, 2026**, with eligible students reclassified by **Jan 31, 2027**. *(Verify locally.)*
- **Student goal-setting conferences** (especially for LTELs in 7th–8th grade): review ELPAC results by domain, set goals, and explain what reclassification means for **high school course access**.
- **Labels have feelings attached:** the Toolkit notes that EL status can carry **socio-emotional and affective impacts**. Students may feel stigmatized. Frame it as **"on the path to biliteracy,"** not a deficit.
- **Monitor RFEP students for 4 years.**

### C6. The Counselor and the Student Study Team (SST)
- Counselors often lead or attend **SSTs**. Use the **"difference vs. disability" questions** from the MLLs with IEPs room: Has the student had good ELD and EL-appropriate interventions? Do the difficulties show up in **both languages**? Has the family been consulted?
- Watch for **LTEL over-referral** to special education.

## PART D: Social-Emotional Support

### D1. Tiered SEL Supports for MLLs
| Tier | Counselor actions |
|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (all)** | Welcoming, **culturally affirming** school climate; SEL lessons that include students' cultures and languages; multilingual signage; **buddy/peer ambassador** programs; staff training on culture shock and trauma |
| **Tier 2 (some)** | **Newcomer groups** (orientation, coping, sharing stories); **LTEL groups** (identity, goal-setting, academic confidence); **regular check-ins** with a trusted adult; **mentoring** |
| **Tier 3 (few)** | Individual counseling; referral to **bilingual / culturally responsive mental health services**; trauma-specific supports (with parent consent); crisis response |

### D2. Newcomer Social-Emotional Supports (U.S. Dept. of Education Newcomer Toolkit)
- **Formal, adult-led:** partnerships with **culturally relevant community-based and faith-based organizations**; newcomer-only sessions; **student check-ins with counselors** to notice changes and build a positive sense of self
- **Peer supports:** buddies, clubs, and peer mentors who speak the student's language
- **Asset-based, trauma-informed teaching:** get to know newcomers **without assumptions** based on culture, language, or country

### D3. Trauma-Informed Practices
- **Safety and predictability:** routines, visual schedules, advance notice of changes (fire drills can be frightening for students who fled violence)
- **Relationships first:** at least one trusted adult who knows the student's name, story (as they choose to share it), and strengths
- **Choice and voice:** let students share their stories when *they* choose; never force it
- **Recognize trauma responses:** withdrawal, irritability, trouble concentrating, and "misbehavior" may be stress responses, not defiance
- **Avoid re-traumatizing practices:** public correction, punitive discipline, and **assignments that require sharing immigration journeys** unless they're optional
- **Self-care for staff:** secondary traumatic stress is real

### D4. Immigration-Related Anxiety
- Students may fear family separation, which affects **attendance, focus, and sleep**.
- **California law protects students** (see Part G): schools may not collect or share immigration status information, and limits apply to immigration enforcement on campus.
- **Counselors should:** know and share the district's policy; know what to do if enforcement officers come to campus; give families "**Know Your Educational Rights**" materials in home languages; help families make **emergency contact / caregiver plans** if they ask; and refer to **trusted legal service organizations**. Counselors **don't give legal advice**.

## PART E: Family Partnership

| Practice | Details |
|---|---|
| **Communicate in the home language** | Use **qualified interpreters** (not students or siblings) for meetings; translate key documents. In California, when **15% or more** of a school's students speak a single primary language other than English, notices must be sent in that language (Ed Code §48985) |
| **Explain the U.S. school system** | Families may not know about grading, GPA, credits, A–G, ELD, ELPAC, reclassification, IEPs, or counselor roles. Hold **family nights in home languages** |
| **Families as experts** | Ask about the student's schooling history, strengths, and goals; honor family aspirations |
| **Flexible access** | Evening and weekend meetings, phone or video options, home visits with a liaison |
| **Community partners** | Partner with community organizations, faith groups, and cultural associations that families already trust |
| **ELAC / DELAC** | Encourage family participation in the **English Learner Advisory Committee** (required for schools with 21 or more ELs) |
| **Build trust around immigration fears** | Share district protections clearly; reassure families that **all children have the right to a free public education regardless of immigration status** (*Plyler v. Doe*, 1982) |

## PART F: College/Career Readiness and Transitions

### F1. Start in Middle School
- **College and career awareness in home languages:** what college is, A–G, financial aid (including California options for undocumented students such as the **California Dream Act**, with general awareness; refer families to specialists)
- **Individual learning plans / goal setting** for every MLL in 7th–8th grade: "I know where I'm going and I know how to get there" (Toolkit)
- **Biliteracy pathway:** recognize home-language skills; heritage/Native Speaker courses; local **biliteracy pathway awards** in middle school leading to the **State Seal of Biliteracy** in high school
- **Career exploration** that **values bilingualism** (interpreting, health care, education, business, public service)

### F2. Middle → High School Transition (Californians Together Toolkit: "Alignment with Middle Schools")
- [ ] A standard **EL Student Profile** for each 8th grader going to high school: ELPAC history, ELD program, interventions, grades, assessment data, program model, IEP status
- [ ] **Articulation meetings** between middle and high school counselors and lead ELD teachers, including **feedback from high school back to middle school**
- [ ] High school program and graduation information **translated** for 8th graders and families
- [ ] **"Welcome to High School" orientation** for EL students and families **in home languages**
- [ ] Placement decisions that avoid **automatic tracking** of ELs into lower math or remedial courses (connects to the Algebra room and the CA Math Placement Act)

### F3. Elementary → Middle School Transition (5th → 6th)
- Gather ELPAC data, typology, and IEP/504 information **before** scheduling
- A middle school orientation for newcomer families (lockers, passing periods, multiple teachers)
- Assign a **peer buddy** for the first weeks

## PART G: California Laws, Standards, and Guidance

| Source | Key points for counselors |
|---|---|
| **California Standards for the School Counseling Profession (CASC)** | Effective counselors **leverage the cultural assets** of diverse students; **address systemic bias**; lead **culturally sustaining** counseling programs; ensure ELs and other marginalized students **are not discriminated against**; promote **culturally responsive, trauma-informed, and restorative** schoolwide practices |
| **CA English Learner Roadmap (2017)** | All four principles apply to counselors: (1) **assets-oriented**, (2) **meaningful access** (placement and scheduling), (3) **system conditions** (counseling systems, data, PD), (4) **alignment and articulation** (transitions) |
| **Education Code §234.7** (AB 699, 2017; amended 2025 by **AB 49, AB 419, AB 495**) | Schools may not collect information on immigration status; they **may not disclose personal information** about students or families to immigration enforcement **without parent consent or a court order**; agents must present proper documentation (a judicial order) to access campuses or records. LEAs must update their policies (AB 49 deadline: **March 1, 2026**). The **CA Attorney General's December 2025 guidance** includes a quick reference for school officials and a "Know Your Educational Rights" guide for families |
| ***Plyler v. Doe* (1982)** | All children have the right to a free public K–12 education **regardless of immigration status** |
| **AB 2555 (CDE guidance)** | ELPAC is the sole reclassification criterion (Overall PL 4). Verify local implementation |
| **Education Code §48985** | Translate notices to parents when 15%+ of students speak a single non-English language |
| **CA Math Placement Act (2015)** | Fair, objective, multiple-measure placement into 9th grade math. English proficiency shouldn't block access |

## PART H: Game Blueprint: The "Counselor's Office" Room

**Room theme idea:** the participant plays a **middle school counselor** with a caseload, an inbox of "cases," and a master schedule board.

| Station (in-room object) | Content source | What the participant does | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Caseload Wall** (hook) | A3 | Sees the ratio challenge | **Visual:** 432 student avatars vs. 250; "Who gets missed?" reflection |
| **2. The Typology Files** | B1 | Identifies EL typologies | **Sort student profile cards** into Newcomer (strong schooling), SIFE, Developing, LTEL, Dually Identified, RFEP; pick one priority action for each |
| **3. The Backpack** | B2–B3 | Understands life issues and stressors | Open a student's "backpack" to reveal stressors (culture shock, family work, separation). Match each to a support |
| **4. The Master Schedule Board** | C1–C3 | Builds an equitable schedule | **Puzzle:** schedule 6 ELs (newcomer, LTEL, SIFE…) into periods while keeping ELD, core classes, **and** electives, and avoiding an "ESL Ghetto." Scored on integration and access |
| **5. The Placement Desk** | C4, F2 | Makes placement decisions | Review a transcript from another country plus ELPAC data → choose a placement; feedback on acceleration vs. remediation |
| **6. The Goal-Setting Conference** | C5 | Holds an LTEL reclassification conference | **Dialogue tree** with an NPC 8th grader: choose asset-based responses; set ELPAC domain goals |
| **7. The SEL Tier Ladder** | D1–D3 | Matches supports to tiers | Place supports (newcomer group, buddy program, bilingual therapist referral) on Tier 1/2/3 |
| **8. The Trauma-Informed Hallway** | D3 | Spots re-traumatizing practices | Walk the hallway; click on 5 practices (a surprise drill with no warning, a forced "tell your immigration story" assignment) and choose better alternatives |
| **9. The Family Night Room** | E | Plans family engagement | Plan a family night: interpreter, timing, topics, community partner. Avoid pitfalls (a student interpreting for their parent) |
| **10. The Know-Your-Rights Bulletin Board** | D4, G | Learns California protections | **Quiz:** "An agent asks the front office for a student's address. What does CA law require?" (written parent consent or a court order) |
| **11. The Bridge to High School** | F1–F2 | Builds a transition plan | Complete an **EL Student Profile** for an 8th grader and plan the orientation |
| **12. Final Challenge: "A Week in the Counselor's Office"** | All | Handles a set of cases | Inbox of 5 cases (a new arrival from Guatemala with interrupted schooling; an LTEL failing classes; a family afraid to come to school; a schedule conflict between ELD and band; an RFEP student slipping). Prioritize and respond to each using the room's tools |
| **13. Exit Door: Commitment** | – | One action for MLLs | Private + public: "To support our MLLs, I (as a counselor/teacher/admin) will…" |

**Room badge:** "MLL Champion Counselor" (or similar), earned after Stations 4 and 12.
**Carry-forward items:** an **EL typology card**, **master scheduling principles**, an **8th grade EL Student Profile template**, and a **Know-Your-Rights quick reference**.
**Connections to other rooms:** MLLs with IEPs (SST, dual services), Grading for Equity (ELPAC/reclassification), Algebra (math placement), Co-Teaching (scheduling partners), Learning Objectives (schoolwide language focus).

## Quick-Reference Card (for participants)

1. **Know your MLLs by typology:** newcomer, SIFE, developing, LTEL, dually identified, RFEP.
2. **Full curriculum access:** core classes, A–G, advanced courses, **and electives**.
3. **Schedule ELD first, then integrate.** Avoid the "ESL Ghetto."
4. **Accelerate, don't remediate.** Time is precious for MLLs.
5. **Goal-set with LTELs** around ELPAC and reclassification. Frame it as a path to biliteracy.
6. **Tiered, trauma-informed, culturally affirming SEL** for all, some, and few.
7. **Families are partners:** qualified interpreters, home-language communication, trusted community partners.
8. **Know California's protections** for immigrant students (Ed Code §234.7, *Plyler*), and share them.
9. **Start college/career and biliteracy planning in middle school.**
10. **Build the bridge:** an EL Student Profile + articulation + orientation for every transition.
