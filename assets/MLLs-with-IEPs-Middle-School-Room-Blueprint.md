# ELD PD Game: Room: Supporting MLLs with IEPs in Middle School

**Topic:** Dually identified students: Multilingual Learners who also have an Individualized Education Program (IEP)
**Suggested campus level:** Middle school room (6th–8th). This is a more advanced topic that builds on earlier rooms:
- **Learning Objectives:** linking language goals to ELD standards
- **Grading for Equity:** accommodations vs. modifications
- **Algebra:** routines that work across content areas
**Main source:** CDE, *California Practitioners' Guide for Educating English Learners with Disabilities* (2019, 464 pages, written in response to AB 2785). Plus current research and policy (see the Sources section).
**Sections:** Part A = who these students are · Part B = difference vs. disability · Part C = the IEP for an English learner · Part D = testing and reclassification · Part E = instruction in middle school · Part F = game room blueprint

---

## PART A: Who Are Our Dually Identified Students?

### A1. California Data (Stanford / Getting Down to Facts, 2014-15 to 2024-25)

| Fact | Number |
|---|---|
| Dually identified students (MLL + disability) in CA | **~177,000 (≈3% of K–12 enrollment)** |
| Share of all MLLs who also receive special education | **~17.5%** |
| Share of all students with disabilities who are also MLLs | **~21.4%** |
| Reclassified as English proficient by grade 12 | **60%** (vs. 75% of MLLs without disabilities) |
| Become **Long-Term English Learners (LTELs)** | **About 1 in 3** (vs. ~12% of MLLs without disabilities) |
| Graduate with a regular diploma | **75%** (vs. 91% of students with neither label) |
| Enroll in college | **44%** (vs. 66%) |

- **Middle school matters:** dually identified students are concentrated in the upper-elementary and middle grades. Historically, LTELs have been placed in special education at **2–4 times the rate** of non-ELs in some CA districts (Californians Together, 2015). In one district, **40% of LTELs** were also in special education.
- **Many of our middle school MLLs with IEPs are LTELs:** socially fluent in English, but stuck in academic English for 6+ years.

### A2. The Core Belief

> **These students are entitled to BOTH:** English language development services **and** special education services. One never replaces the other.

- **Federal guidance:** the 2015 U.S. Dept. of Education / Dept. of Justice *Dear Colleague* letter said EL students with disabilities must receive both language assistance and disability-related services. *(Note: that letter was rescinded in 2025, but the underlying laws, Title VI, IDEA, and the EEOA, still apply.)*
- **California:** the CA Ed.G.E. Initiative regulations made ELD part of **all** language acquisition programs, so ELD must be addressed for **every** English learner with an IEP (CA Practitioners' Guide, Ch. 5).
- **CA EL Roadmap:** assets-oriented, high expectations, meaningful access to grade-level content, for dually identified students too.

## PART B: Language Difference or Disability?

### B1. The Two Mistakes Schools Make
1. **Over-identification:** labeling normal second-language acquisition as a disability (common for LTELs and for Latino MLLs in some districts).
2. **Under-identification / waiting too long:** "Let's wait until their English is better" delays help a student with a real disability needs.

> **California rule:** limited English proficiency **is not** a basis for special education eligibility (CA Practitioners' Guide, Ch. 5).

### B2. Pre-Referral Questions (from the CA Practitioners' Guide)
Before referring an MLL, the student study team should ask:
- [ ] Has the student received **core instruction appropriate for English learners** (integrated + designated ELD)?
- [ ] Has the student received **evidence-based interventions designed for ELs**, implemented with fidelity over time, with little or no progress shown in the data?
- [ ] Does the team have data on the **rate of learning compared to similar EL peers** ("true peers")?
- [ ] Has the team **consulted the parent** about learning patterns and language use at home and in the community?
- [ ] Are **error patterns in the primary language similar to those in English**? If not, are the English errors **typical of second-language learners**?
- [ ] Do the difficulties appear **across settings** (home, school, community)?
- [ ] Have **other explanations** (physical, personal, cultural, learning environment) been ruled out?

### B3. Indicators: Difference vs. Disability (adapted from the CA Practitioners' Guide)

| Behavior | Likely a **language difference** | Possible **disability** |
|---|---|---|
| Doesn't follow verbal directions | Lacks English vocabulary but **understands in L1** | Consistently confused **in both L1 and L2** (processing / cognition) |
| Needs frequent repetition | Understands directions in L1, not yet in L2 | Forgets or needs re-explaining **in L1 and L2, home and school** (auditory memory) |
| Slow to respond | May be **translating in their head**; improves over time | Consistently slow **in both languages; no change over time** (processing speed) |
| Hesitant, halting speech | Still building vocabulary, sentence structure, confidence | Speech hard to understand **in L1 and L2** (speech/hearing) |
| Can't retell a story | Limited English understanding / background knowledge | Difficulty retelling **in L1 and L2** (memory / sequencing) |
| Says very little | Comprehends more than they can express yet | Speaks little **in either language** |
| Forgets letter-sound patterns | Sounds differ between L1 and L2 at first, then improves | Doesn't retain sounds **even ones shared by both languages** |

> **The key test: does the difficulty show up in BOTH languages, across settings, over time?**

### B4. Special Considerations for LTELs (CA Practitioners' Guide, Ch. 2)
- Before moving an LTEL into Tier II/III or special education, ask: **"Is the lack of progress due to the student, or to years of inadequate ELD and core instruction?"**
- Tier I must be **amplified with the CA ELD Standards in every content area**, even while the student gets targeted intervention.
- Look at **LTEL cohort data** at the class, school, and district level. Over-representation of LTELs in intervention or special ed is a **system signal**, not just an individual one.
- **Linguistic segregation and implicit bias** (lowered expectations, separate classes) are among the biggest barriers for ELs and must be addressed directly.

## PART C: The IEP for an English Learner

### C1. What Must Be in the IEP (CA Practitioners' Guide, Ch. 5 checklist)
- [ ] The IEP **identifies the student as an English learner** (or "re-designated," since RFEP students are tracked for 4 years)
- [ ] **Present levels** include current **ELPAC results in all 4 domains** (listening, speaking, reading, writing), and primary-language performance when feasible
- [ ] **ELPAC accessibility resources** for each domain, or an alternate assessment
- [ ] **How ELD will be provided**, both integrated and designated, and **who** provides it
- [ ] Whether **primary language support** is needed
- [ ] The **language of instruction** and the type of language acquisition program
- [ ] **Linguistically Appropriate Goals and Objectives (LAGOS)**

### C2. Linguistically Appropriate Goals (LAGOS)
- **California law:** for students whose primary language is not English, the IEP must include **linguistically appropriate goals, objectives, programs, and services** (**Ed Code §56345(b)(2)**; defined in **5 CCR §3001**).
- LAGOS are **not separate "ELD goals"**. They are IEP goals **aligned with the CA ELD Standards and grade-level standards** that support language acquisition **and** access to the curriculum.
- **Three steps to write LAGOS:**
  1. Review **ELPAC results** to find language areas that need development.
  2. Review **standardized and informal data** to find areas of disability-related need.
  3. **Link** the goal to a **grade-level standard AND a CA ELD standard** at the student's proficiency level.
- **Consider:** cognitive level, linguistic level, L1 and L2 development, progress in designated and integrated ELD, prior knowledge, culturally relevant materials, and the student's heritage.

**Middle school example (from the CA Practitioners' Guide, 7th grade):**

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Grade-level standard** | CCSS L.7.5: Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships, and nuances in word meanings |
| **CA ELD standard** | Part I, Productive mode, **Expanding**: Use a growing set of academic words (e.g., *specific, contrast, significant, function*), domain-specific words (*irony, suspense, analogy, cell membrane, fraction*), synonyms, and antonyms to create precision while speaking and writing |
| **IEP goal (LAGOS)** | By (date), after listening to a teacher-led lecture, (student), given a list of vocabulary words, will **define each word in their own words and write a sentence with an analogy** using it, for **4 out of 5** words in **3 out of 4** vocabulary exercises |

> **Connection to the Learning Objectives room:** a LAGOS is like a long-term language objective. It names the **function** (define, use analogies), the **demand** (academic and domain-specific vocabulary), and the **domain** (listening, writing).

### C3. Family Partnership and Interpreters
- **Ed Code §56341.5(i):** the district must take **any action necessary** to make sure the parent understands the IEP meeting, **including arranging an interpreter** for parents whose native language is not English.
- **Translation of IEP documents:** California law and recent legislation address translating IEPs into parents' native language. **Check current timelines with your SELPA.** Families have reported slow or inaccurate translations (EdSource, 2023).
- **Best practices for interpreters (CA Practitioners' Guide):**
  - The interpreter **sits next to the parent** and interprets at the same time; nobody blocks the parent's view of the speaker
  - **Speak to the parent, not the interpreter**
  - The interpreter translates **everything** and doesn't summarize or add opinions
  - Avoid jargon and acronyms ("IEP," "LRE," "PLOP," "LAGOS") or explain them
  - **Never use the student or a sibling as the interpreter**
  - Treat the family as **experts on their own child**: ask about language use at home, strengths, and history

### C4. Who's at the Table? Collaboration
The CA guide stresses **shared responsibility**: the **special education teacher, ELD teacher/EL specialist, general education teacher, family, and the student** (in middle school, invite the student and build **self-advocacy**). The guide recommends **co-planning and co-teaching** between SPED and ELD staff.

## PART D: Testing and Reclassification

### D1. ELPAC Decisions the IEP Team Makes
| Option | When to use it | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **ELPAC with universal tools / designated supports** | Most dually identified students | Decide **for each domain** |
| **ELPAC with accommodations** | Per IEP/504 | Document in the IEP |
| **Domain exemption** | The disability prevents assessment in a domain even with supports (e.g., a student who is deaf and the Listening domain) | The exempted domain gets the **lowest obtainable scale score**, which can **lower the overall level and affect reclassification**. Usually up to **one domain per composite**. Record it in the IEP |
| **Alternate ELPAC** | **Only** students with the **most significant cognitive disabilities** who take the CAA (alternate CAASPP) | Specific learning disability or speech/language impairment alone **does not qualify**. Use the CDE Alternate Assessment Decision-Making Tool |

### D2. Reclassification Under AB 2555 (CDE guidance)
- The Summative ELPAC is now the **sole criterion**: **Overall Performance Level 4**, or **Overall PL 3 on the Summative Alternate ELPAC**.
- The CDE lists an effective date of **Sept 27, 2026**, with eligible students reclassified by **Jan 31, 2027** based on 2025–26 scores. *(Verify locally, because the CDE notes its guidance is still being updated.)*
- **What it means:** the old local option, where an IEP team could weigh other criteria for a student whose disability affected ELPAC scores, appears to be replaced by the ELPAC-only rule. So **accurate accessibility resources and the right ELPAC decisions in the IEP are more important than ever.**

### D3. Classroom Assessment (from the Grading for Equity room)
- **Accommodations** (extended time, read-aloud, oral response, word banks, graphic organizers) keep the **same standard**. **Grade normally.**
- **Modifications** change the **standard**. **Grade against the modified standard and note it transparently** (Jung & Guskey).
- Separate the **content** grade from **language** growth.

## PART E: Instruction in Middle School

### E1. Evidence-Based Practices

**What Works Clearinghouse practice guide** (*Teaching Academic Content and Literacy to English Learners in Elementary and Middle School*, IES 2014):

| # | Recommendation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Teach a set of **academic vocabulary words intensively** across several days using a variety of activities | **Strong** |
| 2 | **Integrate oral and written English** instruction into content-area teaching | **Strong** |
| 3 | Provide regular, structured opportunities to **develop written language** skills | Minimal |
| 4 | Provide **small-group intervention** for students struggling in literacy and ELD | Moderate |

**Research on middle school ELs with learning disabilities:**
- **Daily interactive vocabulary instruction** (15 minutes a day) helped 6th graders with LD, many of them ELs, **keep the words 4–24 weeks later** (O'Connor et al.).
- Effective content-class practices for dually identified middle schoolers: **build background knowledge, explicit vocabulary instruction, explicit strategy instruction, visual representations, and frequent opportunities to respond** (Hovey et al., 2019).
- Many grades 6–8 ELs with disabilities have **very limited English oral vocabularies**, so **oral language** needs explicit, intensive attention, not just reading skills.
- Reading comprehension: explicit **graphic organizers, vocabulary, and inference** instruction.

### E2. Layering Supports: ELD + UDL + IEP

| Layer | What it looks like in a middle school class |
|---|---|
| **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** (all students) | Multiple ways to **engage** (choice, relevance), **represent** (visuals, video, text-to-speech, bilingual glossaries), and **express** (oral, drawn, typed, recorded) |
| **Integrated ELD** (all ELs) | Language objectives, sentence frames by proficiency level, structured partner talk (e.g., the MLRs from the Algebra room), word walls with cognates |
| **Designated ELD** (ELs, protected time) | Targeted ELD standards instruction. **It must not be skipped for resource or pull-out services.** |
| **IEP supports** (individual) | Accommodations, specialized academic instruction, related services (speech, counseling), and LAGOS progress monitoring |

> **Scheduling watch-out (common in middle school):** a dually identified student should **not** lose designated ELD because they are scheduled into a resource period or SPED elective. Both services must be built into the schedule.

### E3. Classroom Moves for Dually Identified Students
- **Pre-teach vocabulary** with visuals, gestures, examples and non-examples, and L1 cognates
- **Chunk** tasks and texts; give **one step at a time** (written + oral + visual)
- **Model and think aloud**, then guided practice, then independent work (explicit instruction)
- **Graphic organizers** for reading, writing, and math (e.g., the Values/Units chart from the Algebra room)
- **Sentence frames** matched to **both** ELD level and IEP needs
- **Structured talk** with a trusted partner; give **wait time** (remember the "translating in their head" indicator)
- **Allow L1** for thinking, notes, and drafting
- **Frequent, low-stakes checks** for understanding (whiteboards, thumbs, exit tickets)
- **Assistive technology:** text-to-speech, speech-to-text, translation tools as appropriate
- **Social-emotional support:** middle schoolers with two labels may feel stigma. Build belonging, affirm their bilingualism, and teach **self-advocacy** ("I need the directions again," "Can I explain it orally?")

### E4. Collaboration Models (CA Practitioners' Guide, Ch. 6)
- **Co-planning:** the general ed, SPED, and ELD teachers plan together around **both** the IEP goals and the ELD standards
- **Co-teaching:** one teach / one assist, station teaching, parallel teaching, team teaching
- **Shared data:** ELPAC + IEP progress + classroom data reviewed together
- **Least Restrictive Environment (LRE):** dually identified students should be in **general education with peers** (including English-proficient peers) as much as appropriate. Avoid **linguistic segregation**.

## PART F: Game Blueprint: The "MLLs with IEPs" Middle School Classroom

| Station (in-room object) | Content source | What the participant does | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Data Wall** (hook) | A1 | Learns who dually identified students are | **Guess the stat:** guess the % of LTELs, graduation rates, and so on, then reveal |
| **2. The "Both/And" Door** | A2 | Learns that students get BOTH ELD and SPED | Short scenario: "The resource teacher says the student doesn't need ELD anymore." True or false? Explain |
| **3. The Detective's Desk** | B2–B3 | Tells language difference from disability | **Case files:** 4 student profiles with behaviors in L1 and L2. Participant labels each "Language difference / Possible disability / Need more data" and cites evidence |
| **4. The Pre-Referral Checklist** | B2, B4 | Practices the student study team questions | Interactive checklist for an LTEL case. Spot which questions the team skipped |
| **5. The IEP Builder** | C1 | Builds a compliant IEP for an EL | **Drag-and-drop:** put ELPAC scores, ELD services, language of instruction, and LAGOS into the right IEP sections |
| **6. The LAGOS Lab** | C2 | Writes a linguistically appropriate goal | Pick a grade-level standard + ELD standard + ELPAC data → write a measurable goal; compare with the 7th grade example |
| **7. The Family Conference Room** | C3 | Practices interpreter best practices | **Role-play scene:** choose the right moves (seating, who to look at, avoid jargon, don't use a sibling to interpret) |
| **8. The Layered Lesson Board** | E1–E3 | Layers UDL + ELD + IEP supports | Given a 7th grade science lesson, add one support from each layer for a sample student |
| **9. The Schedule Puzzle** | E2, E4 | Fixes a middle school schedule | **Puzzle:** fit designated ELD, resource, and core classes so the student loses no service |
| **10. The Testing Office** | D1–D2 | Makes ELPAC decisions | Decision tree: designated supports, accommodations, domain exemption, or Alternate ELPAC for 3 students; see the reclassification impact |
| **11. Final Challenge: "The Case of Marisol"** | All | Full case study | A 7th grade LTEL with an SLD: review data → decide on supports → write 1 LAGOS → plan 3 classroom moves → prep for the family meeting; self-check with a rubric |
| **12. Exit Door: Commitment** | – | One action for a dually identified student | Private note + public post ("For my dually identified students, I will…") |

**Room badge:** "Both/And Advocate" (or similar), earned after Stations 3 and 11.
**Carry-forward items:** a **Difference vs. Disability indicator card**, an **IEP-for-EL checklist**, and a **LAGOS template**.
**Connections to earlier rooms:** language objectives → LAGOS; Grading for Equity → accommodations vs. modifications; Algebra MLRs → classroom supports in math.

## Quick-Reference Card (for participants)

1. **BOTH, not either/or:** ELD + special education services, always.
2. **Limited English is never a disability.** Check both languages, across settings, over time.
3. **LTEL red flag:** ask whether the student needs a referral, or better instruction first.
4. **The IEP must show:** EL status, ELPAC results, how and who provides ELD, language of instruction, and LAGOS.
5. **LAGOS = IEP goal + ELD standard + grade-level standard.**
6. **Interpreters, not kids,** at IEP meetings. Speak to the parent.
7. **Don't trade designated ELD for resource time.**
8. **Layer it:** UDL for all, ELD for ELs, IEP for the individual.
9. **ELPAC decisions matter more now.** ELPAC is the sole reclassification criterion (AB 2555).
10. **High expectations + grade-level access + inclusion.** Avoid linguistic segregation.
