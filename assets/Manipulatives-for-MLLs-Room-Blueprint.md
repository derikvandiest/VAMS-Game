## PART A: What Are Manipulatives and Why Do They Help MLLs?

### A1. Definition
**Manipulatives** are physical (or virtual) objects students **move, build, and arrange** to model mathematical ideas: algebra tiles, two-color counters, fraction strips, balance scales, unit cubes, geometric solids, and so on. In other subjects, **realia and models** (real objects, cell models, rock samples, artifacts) play a similar role.

### A2. Why They Help MLLs

| Benefit | How it helps an MLL |
|---|---|
| **A shared reference point** | Everyone looks at the **same object**. "This one," plus pointing, works before the precise English word does. The teacher then **attaches the academic term** ("that's a *zero pair*") |
| **Show before tell** | Students can **demonstrate understanding by building** before they can explain in English. That is real evidence of content knowledge (Grading for Equity room) |
| **Lowers the language load, not the rigor** | The math is visible and touchable, so the cognitive energy goes to reasoning while language develops |
| **Gives students something to talk about** | Objects create natural, concrete conversation ("I moved two tiles to the other side because…") |
| **Builds vocabulary in context** | Words like *coefficient, constant, zero pair, face, edge, numerator* are **anchored to an object** instead of a definition |
| **Bridges home and school knowledge** | Many MLLs learned math with different notation or methods. Models reveal the shared **concept** underneath different symbols |
| **Supports students with IEPs** | Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA) sequencing is strongly research-backed for students with learning disabilities, including dually identified MLLs |

> **Research-based recommendation for ELLs** (Chval & Chávez, *Mathematics Teaching in the Middle School*, NCTM): *"Use visual supports such as **concrete objects**, videos, illustrations, and gestures in classroom conversations"* and *"connect language with mathematical representations."*

## PART B: What the Research Says

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Carbonneau, Marley & Selig (2013)**, meta-analysis, *Journal of Educational Psychology*: **55 studies, 7,237 students, K–college** | Manipulatives beat abstract-symbols-only instruction with **small to moderate** effects overall: **moderate to large effects on retention**, **small effects on problem solving, transfer, and justification** |
| ↳ **Instructional guidance** (same study) | For problem solving, **high teacher guidance (d ≈ 1.06)** far outperformed **low guidance (d ≈ 0.04)**. **Manipulatives don't teach by themselves** |
| ↳ **Perceptual richness** (same study) | **Plain, simple objects (d ≈ 0.80)** outperformed **"perceptually rich," realistic or decorative objects (d ≈ 0.27)** on retention. Flashy details can distract from the math |
| ↳ **Topic** (same study) | Largest effects for **fractions**; algebra and geometry were also studied |
| ↳ **Who implements** (same study) | **Teacher-implemented** treatments had larger effects (d ≈ 0.82) than researcher-implemented ones (d ≈ 0.13) |
| **Witzel, Mercer & Miller (2003)**, *Learning Disabilities Research & Practice*: **34 matched pairs of 6th and 7th graders** with LD or at risk in algebra | Students taught to solve **algebra equations** with an explicit **Concrete → Representational → Abstract (CRA)** sequence **outperformed** traditionally taught peers on post-tests **and follow-up**, and made **fewer procedural errors** |
| **IES/WWC practice guides** (*RtI for Mathematics*, 2009; *Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics*, 2021) | Recommend using **visual representations and a well-chosen set of concrete and semi-concrete representations** (manipulatives, number lines, strip/tape diagrams) to teach math concepts (rated **moderate** evidence in the 2009 guide) |
| **Moyer-Packenham & Westenskow (2013; revisited 2016)**, meta-analyses of **virtual manipulatives** | Virtual manipulatives showed an overall **moderate positive effect (≈0.34)** compared with other instructional approaches |
| **CA Mathematics Framework (2023), Ch. 7: Grades 6–8** | Math discourse includes communicating "with words, gestures, drawings, **manipulatives**, representations, symbols, and other tools." Students can show understanding when they "**build a concrete model**." The framework also recommends small-group instruction for English learners to build the language for upcoming lessons |

> **Honest summary:** manipulatives **help, especially for retention and for students who struggle**, but **only with strong teacher guidance and explicit connections to symbols**. EL-specific experimental research is limited. The case for MLLs rests on general manipulatives research **plus** well-supported EL principles: visuals, gestures, multiple representations, and language as a resource (Moschkovich; Chval & Chávez).

## PART C: Best Practices for Middle School

### C1. Use the CRA Sequence (Concrete → Representational → Abstract)

```
 CONCRETE                  REPRESENTATIONAL               ABSTRACT
 (build it)        ──►     (draw it)              ──►     (write it)
 algebra tiles             sketch of tiles / boxes        2x + 3 = 7
 on the mat                and circles                    2x = 4
                                                          x = 2
 "Show me"                 "Draw it"                      "Write it"
```
- **Don't stay concrete too long, and don't skip the middle.** The *representational* (drawing) stage is the bridge most often skipped.
- **Connect all three explicitly:** "Where is the 3 in your tiles? In your drawing? In the equation?" (This is MLR7, Compare and Connect, from the Algebra room.)
- Over time, students **choose** whether they need the tool. That builds independence.

### C2. Ten Best Practices
1. **Teach with high guidance.** Model with a think-aloud first, then guided practice, then independent work. Free exploration alone doesn't build understanding.
2. **Choose simple, plain manipulatives.** Avoid novelty or "cute" objects that distract (Carbonneau et al., 2013).
3. **Always connect to symbols and words.** Concrete → drawing → equation, every time.
4. **Pair every action with language.** Students **say what they do** ("I'm adding a zero pair") with frames and a word bank.
5. **Set norms and routines.** Distribute, use, and clean up efficiently. Give 1–2 minutes of free exploration first so curiosity doesn't hijack the lesson.
6. **Make them "grown-up" for middle schoolers.** Call them **tools or models** ("engineers and architects use models"). Use virtual versions, which teens often prefer, and offer choice to avoid stigma ("these are for little kids").
7. **Make them available to everyone,** not just MLLs or students with IEPs. That's Universal Design for Learning and avoids labeling.
8. **Label the tools bilingually.** Bin and mat labels in English + students' home languages, plus cognates (*tile / teja*, *cube / cubo*, *fraction / fracción*).
9. **Use them for talk and assessment.** Students **build and explain**. The teacher observes and records language and understanding (co-teaching Model #3).
10. **Plan the language objective too.** For example: "Students will **explain** how to solve a two-step equation **using the terms *zero pair, coefficient, constant*** **by narrating their tile moves to a partner**."

### C3. Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Fun day" with no math goal | Tie every use to a **specific concept and objective** |
| Students follow steps mechanically ("move tiles") without understanding | Ask "**why**" questions; have students **predict** before moving |
| Never moving to symbols | Plan the **R** and **A** stages; record each step in equations |
| Teacher demonstrates, students watch | **Students' hands on the tools**; the teacher circulates |
| Manipulatives only for "low" students | Offer them to **all**; advanced students use them to justify and generalize |
| Distracting, realistic objects | Use **plain, consistent** tools |
| No language support | Use **frames, word banks, and labels**; narrate moves |

## PART D: 10 Middle School Manipulative Examples

Each example includes the **concept, how it works, language targets, and MLL supports**. (CCSS grade-level standards in parentheses.)

---

### D1. Algebra Tiles: Solving Equations (6.EE.7, 7.EE.4, 8.EE.7)
**Problem:** 2x + 3 = 7

```
 LEFT SIDE                    │   RIGHT SIDE
 [  x  ] [  x  ] ■ ■ ■         │   ■ ■ ■ ■ ■ ■ ■
                               │
 Step 1: remove 3 unit tiles from BOTH sides (keep it balanced)
 [  x  ] [  x  ]               │   ■ ■ ■ ■
 Step 2: split into 2 equal groups
 [  x  ]                       │   ■ ■
 [  x  ]                       │   ■ ■      →   x = 2
```
**CRA:** build with tiles → sketch rectangles and squares → write the equations step by step
**Vocabulary:** *variable, constant, coefficient, equation, both sides, balance, isolate, equal groups*
**Frames:**
- **E:** "I take away ___ from both sides."
- **X:** "I removed ___ units from both sides to keep the equation balanced."
- **B:** "To isolate the variable, I subtracted ___ from both sides, then divided into ___ equal groups because the coefficient is ___."
**MLL tip:** a cognate word wall (*ecuación, variable, constante, coeficiente*); pair students to narrate each other's moves.

---

### D2. Two-Color Counters: Integer Operations (7.NS.1, 7.NS.2)
**Problem:** −3 + 5

```
 ● = +1 (yellow)     ○ = −1 (red)
 ○ ○ ○   +   ● ● ● ● ●
 Pair them up: (○●) (○●) (○●) ● ●   → 3 ZERO PAIRS cancel out
 Result: ● ●  =  +2
```
**Key idea:** a **zero pair** (+1 and −1) equals 0
**Vocabulary:** *positive, negative, zero pair, opposite, integer, sum, difference*
**Frames:**
- **E:** "I make ___ zero pairs. ___ are left."
- **X:** "When I combine ___ and ___, ___ zero pairs cancel, so the sum is ___."
- **B:** "Since each zero pair has a value of zero, removing them shows that ___."
**Extension:** subtraction (−2 − (−5)) by **adding zero pairs** so there's something to take away.

---

### D3. Balance Scale (Physical or Virtual): Equations and Equality (6.EE.5, 6.EE.7)
**Idea:** the **equal sign means "balanced,"** not "the answer comes next"
**Activity:** put mystery bags (x) and unit weights on each side; whatever you do to one side, you **must** do to the other.
**Vocabulary:** *equal, balanced, unbalanced, both sides, solve*
**Frames:** "The scale is balanced because ___." / "If I remove ___ from one side, I must ___."
**MLL tip:** the **gesture** of two hands as a scale becomes a shared signal for "equal / keep it balanced."

---

### D4. Fraction Strips / Fraction Tiles + Number Line (6.NS.1, 7.NS.1)
**Problem:** 3/4 ÷ 1/8, i.e., "How many 1/8s fit in 3/4?"

```
 1/4 1/4 1/4                 (3/4)
 ⅛ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛                  six 1/8 pieces fit  →  3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 6
```
**Vocabulary:** *numerator, denominator, equivalent, unit fraction, how many … fit in*
**Frames:** "___ pieces of ___ fit in ___, so ___ ÷ ___ = ___."
**Research note:** fractions showed the **largest manipulatives effect** in the Carbonneau meta-analysis. Chval & Chávez describe teachers using **fraction strips and number lines** to uncover ELLs' prior knowledge without words.

---

### D5. Hundred Grids / Percent Bars: Percents, Decimals, Ratios (6.RP.3c, 7.RP.3)
**Problem:** what is 30% of 60?

```
 0%      10%     20%     30%  ...                 100%
 |-------|-------|-------|-------- ... ------------|
 0       6       12      18                         60
                         ↑ 30% of 60 = 18
```
**Tools:** a hundred grid (shade 30 squares = 0.30 = 30%), a percent bar / double number line
**Vocabulary:** *percent, out of 100, part, whole, discount, tax, tip*
**Frames:** "___% of ___ is ___ because ___." / "The whole is ___ and the part is ___."
**MLL tip:** **real-life context** with realia: a store receipt, a sale flyer (in English and Spanish), a menu.

---

### D6. Unit Cubes and Nets: Volume and Surface Area (6.G.2, 6.G.4, 7.G.6)
**Activity:** build a rectangular prism from unit cubes (volume = how many cubes fill it); unfold a cardboard box or paper **net** (surface area = area of all the faces)
**Vocabulary:** *face, edge, vertex, base, height, volume, surface area, net, cubic units, square units*
**Frames:** "The prism has ___ faces." / "Volume measures ___, but surface area measures ___."
**MLL tip:** students **touch** each face as they say *face*, run a finger along an *edge*, and point to a *vertex* (Total Physical Response). Spanish cognates: *volumen, área, vértice, base*.

---

### D7. Spinners, Dice, Colored Chips: Probability (7.SP.5–7.SP.8)
**Activity:** predict, then run 20–50 trials; compare **experimental** vs. **theoretical** probability
**Vocabulary:** *likely, unlikely, certain, impossible, outcome, trial, probability, equally likely*
**Frames:** "It is ___ likely that ___ because ___." / "We predicted ___, but the results showed ___."
**MLL tip:** a **likelihood line** (impossible → certain) with pictures. Note that games with dice and cards may already be familiar from home cultures, which builds on an asset.

---

### D8. Algebra Tiles: Distributive Property and Combining Like Terms (6.EE.3, 7.EE.1)
**Problem:** 3(x + 2)

```
 3 rows of  [ x ] ■ ■
            [ x ] ■ ■
            [ x ] ■ ■
 → 3 x-tiles + 6 unit tiles = 3x + 6
```
**Vocabulary:** *distribute, expression, like terms, equivalent, factor, product*
**Frames:** "___ groups of ___ is the same as ___." / "These expressions are equivalent because ___."
**MLL tip:** *like terms* is a multiple-meaning word (see the Algebra room). The tiles show **"same shape = like terms."**

---

### D9. Pattern Blocks / Linking Cubes: Growing Patterns → Functions (8.F.1–8.F.4)
**Activity:** build Figure 1, 2, 3 of a growing pattern; record in a **table**; find the rule (y = 3x + 1); graph it
**Vocabulary:** *pattern, term, figure number, rate of change, input, output, function*
**Frames:** "Each figure adds ___ cubes, so the rate of change is ___." / "The rule is ___ because ___."
**Connection:** concrete pattern → table → graph → equation (the four representations from the Algebra room)

---

### D10. Beyond Math: Realia and Models in Science and Social Studies
| Subject | Manipulative / realia | Concept | Language target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Science (6th) | Rock and mineral samples, hand lenses | Rock types, properties | Describe: *texture, layers, crystals* |
| Science (7th) | 3D cell model or a cell "build" kit | Organelles and functions | Explain: "The ___ is like a ___ because it ___." |
| Science (8th) | Marbles/beads as atoms; molecule kits | Atoms, molecules, reactions | Sequence/cause: "When ___ combines with ___, it forms ___." |
| Social studies | Artifact replicas, primary-source photos, 3D maps, timelines with cards | Ancient civilizations, geography | Compare/infer: "This object suggests ___ because ___." |
| ELA | Sentence-building cards / word tiles | Sentence structure, combining clauses (ELD Part II) | "I joined these ideas with *because* / *although*." |

## PART E: Language Routines with Manipulatives

### E1. "Show It → Say It → Draw It → Write It"
1. **Show it:** build the model with the manipulative
2. **Say it:** explain to a partner using frames and a word bank (oral rehearsal)
3. **Draw it:** sketch the model (representational)
4. **Write it:** record the equation **and** one sentence explaining it

### E2. Partner Narration (Builder / Recorder)
- **Builder** moves the tiles and **narrates each move**: "I'm removing 3 units from both sides."
- **Recorder** writes the matching equation step and asks "**Why** did you do that?"
- **Switch roles** each problem.
- Works great with **MLR1 Stronger & Clearer** and **MLR8 Discussion Supports**.

### E3. Information Gap with Manipulatives (MLR4)
Partner A builds a hidden model (behind a folder). Partner B must **recreate it using only Partner A's spoken description**. This forces precise language ("Put **two** *x*-tiles on the **left** side…").

### E4. Manipulatives Word Wall
Post a photo of each tool with its **name in English and home languages**, the **math vocabulary it teaches**, and a **sample sentence frame**.

### E5. Language Frames by Proficiency Level

| Function | Emerging | Expanding | Bridging |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Describe the model** | "I have ___ tiles." | "My model shows ___ groups of ___." | "This model represents ___ because each ___ stands for ___." |
| **Explain a step** | "I move ___." | "I ___ both sides so that ___." | "To keep the equation balanced, I ___, which ___." |
| **Connect to symbols** | "This is ___." (pointing) | "The ___ in my model is the ___ in the equation." | "Each step with the tiles corresponds to ___ in the equation because ___." |
| **Justify** | "It is ___ because ___." | "I know my answer is correct because when I ___." | "My solution is valid since ___; I can verify it by ___." |

## PART F: Virtual Manipulatives

**Why use them in middle school:** no clean-up, no lost pieces; teens often see them as more "grown-up"; they work for **remote and hybrid** learning; many have **built-in links** between the model and symbols; and they **support IEP accommodations** (zoom, color, audio).
**Research:** overall **moderate positive effects** (Moyer-Packenham & Westenskow, 2013/2016).

| Tool | Free? | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Polypad** (Mathigon, now part of Amplify) | Free, no login | Algebra tiles, balance scales, fraction bars, number lines, geometry. Designed for middle and high school |
| **Desmos Classroom** (Activity Builder) | Free | Graphing, functions; teacher-built activities; pairs with Polypad |
| **Didax Virtual Manipulatives** | Free | Algebra tiles, integer chips, fraction tiles |
| **Mathsbot** | Free | Algebra tiles and disks, integer counters |
| **National Library of Virtual Manipulatives** (Utah State) | Free | Classic collection *(check current availability)* |
| **Illustrative Math** digital applets | Included with the curriculum | Built-in manipulatives in IM lessons |

**MLL tips for virtual tools:** set the interface language when available; pair the virtual tool with the **same frames** used for physical tools; use **screenshots** of student models as evidence in portfolios.

> **Best practice:** use **physical first** when introducing a concept (touch + gesture + talk), then **virtual** for practice, homework, and showing work.

## PART G: California Connections

- **CA Mathematics Framework (2023), Ch. 7 (Grades 6–8):**
  - Mathematical discourse includes communicating "with words, gestures, drawings, **manipulatives**, representations, symbols, and other tools."
  - Students show deeper understanding when they "express an idea in their own words, **build a concrete model**, illustrate their thinking pictorially, and/or provide examples."
  - "The same representations and **concrete objects** used as models of real-life situations are used to understand mathematical or statistical concepts."
  - Because the Standards for Mathematical Practice are "linguistically demanding," teachers must support students "who may grasp a concept yet **struggle to express their understanding**." **Small-group instruction** is recommended for English learners to build the language for upcoming lessons.
- **CA Math Framework, Ch. 2:** teachers should notice how students use **translanguaging, gestures, and representations** to express math ideas, and **attend to ideas rather than correcting vocabulary** (Moschkovich).
- **CA ELD Standards:** manipulative talk directly practices **Part I, Collaborative** (exchanging ideas), **Interpretive** (listening actively), and **Productive** (explaining, justifying), plus **Part II** (using precise verbs and nouns, connecting ideas with *because, so, then*).
- **CA ELD Proficiency Level Descriptors:** Emerging students can **"express ideas using visuals such as drawings, charts, or graphic organizers"** and **"use gestures to communicate basic information."** Manipulatives give them a concrete way to participate in grade-level math.
- **Universal Design for Learning (CA Math Framework & CA Practitioners' Guide):** multiple means of **representation** and **expression**. Manipulatives serve both.
- **IEP accommodations:** manipulatives and virtual tools are common accommodations. CRA is especially effective for students with math learning disabilities (Witzel et al., 2003).

## PART H: Game Blueprint: The "Manipulatives" Classroom

**Room theme idea:** a "**Maker Lab**" or "**Math Workshop**" with workbenches, tool bins, and a "Build-Draw-Write" conveyor belt.

| Station (in-room object) | Content source | What the participant does | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Mystery Box** (hook) | A2 | Experiences learning without shared words | A short math task given only in an unfamiliar language, first with no tools, then with tiles and gestures. Reflect on the difference |
| **2. The Research Bench** | B | Learns what the evidence says | **True/False flip cards:** "Fancier, more realistic manipulatives work better" → False (d ≈ 0.27 vs. 0.80) |
| **3. The CRA Conveyor Belt** | C1 | Sequences Concrete → Representational → Abstract | Drag the tile model, the drawing, and the equation onto the belt in order, then match each step across all three |
| **4. The Tool Bin Matcher** | D1–D10 | Matches tools to concepts | **Drag-and-drop:** two-color counters → integers; unit cubes → volume; percent bar → discounts |
| **5. The Algebra Tile Workbench** | D1, D8 | Solves equations with virtual algebra tiles | Interactive: solve 2x + 3 = 7 and 3(x + 2) with tiles; each move must be **narrated by choosing the right sentence frame** |
| **6. The Zero Pair Challenge** | D2 | Integer operations with counters | Mini-game: cancel zero pairs to solve 5 integer problems against a timer |
| **7. The Word Wall Builder** | C2 #8, E4 | Labels tools bilingually | Match each tool photo to its English name, a cognate, the vocabulary it teaches, and a frame |
| **8. The Partner Narration Booth** | E2–E3 | Practices Builder/Recorder and Info Gap | With an NPC partner: describe a hidden model precisely enough for them to rebuild it |
| **9. The Pitfall Workshop** | C3 | Fixes bad manipulative lessons | Scenarios: "Students played with cubes all period; no equations were written." Choose the fix |
| **10. The Virtual Tool Lab** | F | Explores free virtual tools | Try Polypad / Didax in an embedded frame; pick the best tool for 3 lesson goals |
| **11. Final Challenge: "Design a Hands-On Lesson"** | All | Plans a full CRA lesson | Choose a 6–8 standard → pick a manipulative → plan C-R-A steps, a language objective, frames for 2 levels, and a talk routine → self-check with the C2 best-practices list |
| **12. Exit Door: Commitment** | – | One tool to try next week | Private + public: "Next week I'll use ___ to teach ___." |

**Room badge:** "Master Builder" (or similar), earned after Stations 3 and 11.
**Carry-forward items:** a **CRA planning card**, a **manipulative-to-concept chart**, and **frames for narrating moves**.
**Connections to other rooms:** Algebra (tiles, balance, four representations), Graphic Organizers (the representational stage), Learning Objectives (language objective for a hands-on lesson), MLLs with IEPs (CRA as an accommodation), Co-Teaching (stations), Grading for Equity (a built model as evidence of understanding).

## Quick-Reference Card (for participants)

1. **Concrete → Representational → Abstract.** Don't skip the drawing.
2. **High guidance:** model it, then guide it, then release it. Tools don't teach by themselves.
3. **Plain beats fancy.** Simple tools work better than realistic ones.
4. **Hands on tools, words in mouths:** show it, say it, draw it, write it.
5. **Connect every move to symbols and vocabulary.**
6. **Label tools bilingually**; use gestures and cognates.
7. **For everyone,** not just "struggling" students. Make them grown-up with the "tools engineers use" framing.
8. **Physical first, virtual for practice** (Polypad, Desmos, Didax).
9. **Write a language objective** for every hands-on lesson.
10. **A model a student built is evidence of learning.** Accept it.
