# ELD PD Game: Room: How Co-Teaching Supports Our MLLs

**Topic:** Co-teaching between content teachers and ELD/EL specialists (and special education teachers) to serve Multilingual Learners

## PART A: What Is Co-Teaching for MLLs?

### A1. Definition
**Co-teaching** = **two certified teachers** (e.g., a content teacher + an ELD/EL specialist, or a general ed + special ed teacher) **sharing responsibility** for planning, teaching, assessing, and reflecting on **one group of students in the same classroom**.

For MLLs, the main goal is to **develop language and literacy while building content knowledge**, so students don't have to choose between learning English and learning grade-level content (Dove & Honigsfeld).

### A2. Why It Helps MLLs
- **Two kinds of expertise in one room:** the content teacher knows the *destination* (standards, concepts). The ELD specialist knows the *road* (language demands, scaffolds). (Remember the Learning Objectives room: "If the content is the destination, the language demand is the road.")
- **Access to grade-level content** with peers, instead of being pulled out and missing core instruction (CA EL Roadmap Principle 2).
- **Lower adult-to-student ratio** → more talk, more feedback, more small-group work.
- **Less linguistic segregation:** MLLs learn alongside English-proficient peers.
- **Professional growth:** content teachers learn language strategies, and ELD teachers deepen their content knowledge. The benefit lasts beyond the co-taught class.

### A3. What Co-Teaching Is NOT
- ❌ The ELD teacher acting as a **classroom aide** or "helper"
- ❌ "Push-in" where the specialist **sits beside one or two MLLs** whispering translations
- ❌ One teacher teaching while the other **grades papers or makes copies**
- ❌ Taking turns ("You teach Monday, I'll teach Tuesday")
- ❌ A **replacement for designated ELD** (in California, designated ELD is still required)

## PART B: The Co-Teaching Models

### B1. Dove & Honigsfeld's Seven Models (designed specifically for MLLs)
*From* Co-Teaching for English Learners *(Corwin) and the NYSED Topic Brief "Seven Models of Co-Teaching."* Most models should **not** run for a whole class period. Pick them to match the **lesson objectives and student needs**.

| # | Model | What it looks like | Best for MLLs when… | Watch out for… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **One Group: One Leads, One "Teaches on Purpose"** | One teacher leads; the other gives **targeted mini-lessons**, checks understanding, clarifies directions, and scaffolds on the spot | Launching a lesson, activating prior knowledge, modeling a strategy (**5–10 min**) | It becomes the "**no-planning default**" where the specialist turns into a helper. **Rotate the lead** |
| **2** | **One Group: Two Teach the Same Content** | Both teachers lead together, e.g., **modeling a dialogue** or a think-aloud | Modeling academic conversations and language functions (analyze, predict, argue) | Needs the **most detailed co-planning** and smooth handoffs |
| **3** | **One Group: One Teaches, One Assesses** | One teaches; the other **collects data** with a checklist or observation focus (e.g., MLL language use during discussion) | Formative assessment of **language and content**, IEP/LAGOS progress | Don't overuse it; plan together so it isn't "one teacher's lesson" |
| **4** | **Two Groups: Two Teach the Same Content** | Split the class; each teacher teaches **the same lesson** to a smaller group (*parallel teaching*) | More talk time and risk-taking for MLLs; can differentiate the language scaffolds | Noise; both teachers must be confident with the content |
| **5** | **Two Groups: One Pre-teaches, One Teaches Alternative Information** | One group gets **front-loaded vocabulary/background knowledge**; the other gets enrichment or related content | Building background and vocabulary **before** a hard text or task | **Stigma** if the same MLLs are always pulled. Use **flexible grouping** (by content need, not only ELPAC level) |
| **6** | **Two Groups: One Reteaches, One Teaches Alternative Information** | One group gets **re-teaching/review**; the other extends or enriches | After a formative check shows a gap | Labeling; groups may need different amounts of time |
| **7** | **Multiple Groups: Two Monitor and Teach** | **Stations / centers** (3–8 groups); both teachers facilitate | Using **all 4 domains**, tiered tasks, lots of peer interaction | Needs heavy planning; avoid permanent homogeneous (segregated) groups |

### B2. The Classic Six Approaches (Friend & Cook; used in special education and in the CA Practitioners' Guide)

| Approach | Matches Dove & Honigsfeld model |
|---|---|
| One Teach, One Observe | #3 One Teaches, One Assesses |
| One Teach, One Assist | #1 One Leads, One Teaches on Purpose |
| Team Teaching | #2 Two Teach the Same Content |
| Parallel Teaching | #4 Two Groups, Same Content |
| Alternative (Supplemental) Teaching | #5 Pre-teach / #6 Reteach |
| Station Teaching | #7 Multiple Groups |

> **California note:** The CA Practitioners' Guide (Fig. 6.11) lists these models and states that **"both integrated and designated ELD instruction are a part of every model."**

### B3. Choosing a Model: Quick Decision Guide

| If your lesson goal is… | Try… |
|---|---|
| Introduce new content or model a strategy | #1 (rotate the lead) or #2 |
| Model academic talk / a language function | #2 Two Teach (scripted dialogue) |
| Gather formative data on MLL language use | #3 One Teaches, One Assesses |
| Get every student talking | #4 Parallel or #7 Stations |
| Front-load vocabulary before a complex text | #5 Pre-teach |
| Close a gap from yesterday's exit ticket | #6 Reteach |
| Practice in all 4 domains / differentiate | #7 Stations |

## PART C: The Collaborative Instructional Cycle

Co-teaching is more than sharing a room. Dove & Honigsfeld frame it as a **four-part cycle**:

```
   1. CO-PLANNING  ──►  2. CO-INSTRUCTION
         ▲                       │
         │                       ▼
   4. REFLECTION  ◄──  3. CO-ASSESSMENT
```

| Phase | What partners do together | MLL focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Co-Planning** | Unpack standards; write **content + language objectives**; choose a model; plan scaffolds and groupings | Name the **language function, demand, and domain** (Learning Objectives formula); anticipate the language challenges in texts and tasks; plan frames by ELD level |
| **2. Co-Instruction** | Teach using the chosen model(s); share the lead; both teachers work with **all** students | Use MLRs (Algebra room), structured talk, visuals, and L1 support |
| **3. Co-Assessment** | Design and score assessments together; collect formative data | **Two-row rubric** (content + language), as in the Grading for Equity room; track ELD and IEP/LAGOS progress |
| **4. Reflection** | Debrief: what worked, what didn't, which students need what next | Look at MLL work samples; adjust groups and scaffolds; celebrate growth |

### C1. A Co-Planning Protocol (about 30–45 minutes a week)
1. **Content teacher shares** the upcoming unit, standards, key tasks, and assessments.
2. **ELD/EL specialist identifies** the language demands: vocabulary, sentence structures, text features, discourse (explain, argue, compare).
3. **Together:** write the content + language objectives for key lessons.
4. **Choose co-teaching models** for each lesson segment.
5. **Plan scaffolds** by proficiency level (Emerging / Expanding / Bridging) and IEP needs.
6. **Agree on co-assessment:** what evidence, which rubric, who collects what.
7. **Divide prep tasks fairly** (parity!).

### C2. Sample Co-Taught Lesson (7th grade science: "Explain how energy flows in an ecosystem")

| Time | Model | Content teacher | ELD teacher |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8 min | #2 Two Teach | Both model a **dialogue** using "___ gets energy from ___ because ___" with a food web visual | ← (shared) |
| 8–20 min | #5 Pre-teach / Alt. | Group B: extends to energy pyramids | Group A (flexible): **front-loads** *producer, consumer, decomposer* with visuals, cognates (*productor, consumidor*), and gestures |
| 20–40 min | #7 Stations | Station 1: food web card sort | Station 2: **structured partner talk** (MLR1 Stronger & Clearer); Station 3: independent reading with glossary |
| 40–50 min | #3 One Teaches, One Assesses | Leads the exit-ticket explanation | **Observes and records** MLL language use with a checklist |
| After class | **Co-assess and reflect** | Scores content row | Scores language row; together they regroup students for tomorrow |

## PART D: What the Research Says

### D1. Co-Teaching for ELD: 2026 Systematic Review
**Burho, Woodruff, Koetje, Sandstead, Thompson, Kibler & Bovee (2026).** *Co-teaching in English Language Development: A Systematic Review.* National R&D Center to Improve Education for Secondary English Learners, Oregon State University. **40 studies (2000–2025).**

**Key findings:**
- **Foundations:** Effective partnerships depend on **trust, communication, voluntary participation, protected co-planning time, and administrative leadership that values the ELD teacher's expertise**. When administrators assign **large caseloads, disrupt schedules, or treat ELD teachers as support staff**, collaboration weakens and role inequities persist.
- **Ideology matters:** schools often treat language development as **"supplementary"** to content. That **marginalizes ELD expertise**. When co-teachers treat **language development as core academic work**, partnerships show more **parity** and more meaningful **inclusion**.
- **Implementation:** structured **co-planning and co-reflection** help teachers align **language and content objectives**. But planning structures alone don't guarantee parity. When language objectives stay peripheral, ELD teachers end up as **assistants**, and **whole-class teaching led by the content teacher** remains common.
- **Impact:** evidence on student outcomes is **still limited**. Only **5 studies** examined impact directly. One controlled study found **small positive gains in some English proficiency domains**; others showed positive trends or more **participation**, without causal proof.
- **Bottom line:** *"Co-teaching alone is unlikely to yield consistent improvements without integration of language-development expertise into instruction."*

### D2. Other Findings
- **Equity in practice:** In a study of 20 co-taught classes, **70%** gave MLLs support **at or above** their share of the class population. Supportive strategies appeared in **53%** of observed intervals.
- **Special education co-teaching** (broader research base): Murawski & Swanson's (2001) meta-analysis found a **moderate positive effect** overall, with similar cautions about study quality. *(Cited from general knowledge.)*
- **Teacher learning:** co-teaching consistently shows **professional growth** for both partners. Content teachers adopt language strategies, and ELD teachers gain content depth.
- **Philadelphia (2020):** after a PD model with **structured co-planning**, co-planning became **more frequent** among partners.

> **Takeaway for the PD:** co-teaching is a **promising structure, not a magic bullet**. It works when **both teachers' expertise drives instruction**, with planned language objectives, real parity, and protected planning time.

## PART E: Making It Work, and Common Pitfalls

### E1. Common Pitfalls (from the research)

| Pitfall | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **"Glorified aide"** | The ELD teacher circulates, translates, or makes copies | **Rotate the lead**; the ELD teacher leads the language portions; both names on the door and the syllabus |
| **No planning time** | Partners "wing it," defaulting to Model #1 | **Protected weekly co-planning** in the master schedule |
| **Too many partners** | The ELD specialist is spread across 5–6 teachers in different subjects | Limit caseloads (MA DESE guidance suggests **1–3 co-teaching partnerships**, balanced with planning time) |
| **Language as an afterthought** | Content objective only; no language objective | **Co-write language objectives** every lesson |
| **Static ability groups** | The same MLLs are always in the "pre-teach" group | **Flexible grouping** by content need, interest, and language goal |
| **Unequal roles in students' eyes** | Students see one "real teacher" | Introduce both as **co-teachers**; both give directions, feedback, and grades |
| **Personality conflict / no trust** | Avoidance, tension | Norms and agreements up front; voluntary pairing when possible; regular reflection |
| **Replacing designated ELD** | "They get ELD in the co-taught class" | In CA, **designated ELD is still required** in addition to integrated ELD |

### E2. Partnership Agreements (CA Practitioners' Guide, Ch. 6)
- **Set and respect dates:** schedule co-planning and data meetings in advance
- **Make and stick to a plan:** decide when co-teaching happens and how monitoring works
- **Trust one another:** talk openly about successes and challenges
- **Focus on the students:** share **IEP goals** and **ELD levels** with every partner; build lessons around specific needs
- **Monitor with data:** observation notes, writing samples, periodic assessments

### E3. Parity Signals Checklist
- [ ] Both teachers' names on the door, syllabus, and parent communication
- [ ] Both teachers lead instruction during the week
- [ ] Both teachers have a desk/space and materials in the room
- [ ] Both give grades and feedback to **all** students (not "your kids / my kids")
- [ ] Planning and prep tasks are split fairly
- [ ] Language objectives are posted and taught **as core content**
- [ ] Both attend parent conferences and IEP meetings for shared students

### E4. What Administrators Must Provide
- **Protected co-planning time** in the master schedule
- **Reasonable caseloads** for ELD specialists
- **Consistent partnerships** (keep pairs together across years when possible)
- **Joint professional learning** for content, ELD, and SPED teachers together (the CA Practitioners' Guide recommends this)
- **Clear role descriptions** that position the ELD teacher as a **co-teacher, not support staff**
- **Scheduling that clusters MLLs** thoughtfully in co-taught sections **without creating segregated classes**

## PART F: California Connections

- **Integrated + Designated ELD:** co-teaching is a strong way to deliver **integrated ELD** (ELD standards used *together with* content standards in every subject). It **does not replace designated ELD**, which is protected time focused on the ELD standards (ELA/ELD Framework). The CA Practitioners' Guide notes that both are **part of every model**.
- **CA EL Roadmap:**
  - **Principle 2 (Meaningful Access):** grade-level content with scaffolds, inside the core classroom
  - **Principle 3 (System Conditions):** leadership, collaboration time, and professional learning
  - **Principle 4 (Alignment):** consistent support across classrooms and grades
- **CA Practitioners' Guide for ELs with Disabilities (2019), Ch. 6:**
  - General education, EL specialists, and special education teachers **share responsibility**: SPED teachers monitor IEPs with input from general ed, and general ed teachers monitor **ELD progress** with SPED support
  - Recommends **joint professional learning** for all three groups on culturally and linguistically responsive teaching and integrated/designated ELD
  - Gives sample models where SPED teachers **push in to co-teach integrated ELD** in science, and where SDC and general ed teachers co-teach several times a week
- **Least Restrictive Environment + linguistic integration:** co-teaching keeps dually identified students and MLLs **in general education with peers**, supporting both IDEA's LRE and the Roadmap's stance against linguistic segregation.

## PART G: Game Blueprint: The "Co-Teaching" Classroom

**Special mechanic idea:** this room could be the game's first **partner room**. Two participants enter together, or a participant is paired with an NPC "co-teacher" character. Some stations require both players.

| Station (in-room object) | Content source | What the participant does | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Two-Desk Doorway** (hook) | A1–A3 | Sees "real" vs. "fake" co-teaching | **Spot the difference:** two classroom animations, one where the ELD teacher acts as an aide and one with true co-teaching. Identify 3 differences |
| **2. The Is / Is Not Wall** | A3 | Sorts co-teaching examples | **Drag-and-drop:** "ELD teacher translates for 2 students in the back" → Is Not |
| **3. The Model Gallery** | B1–B2 | Learns the 7 models | **Seven floor tiles:** step on each to see the room rearrange (one group / two groups / stations) with a short description and an MLL use case |
| **4. The Model Matcher** | B3 | Chooses the right model | Given 6 lesson goals ("front-load vocabulary before a complex text"), pick a model and justify the choice |
| **5. The Planning Table** | C, C1 | Practices co-planning | **Two-role puzzle:** the content-teacher card gives the standard and task; the ELD-teacher card gives the language demands. Combine them into a content + language objective (uses the Learning Objectives formula card) |
| **6. The Lesson Timeline Builder** | C2 | Builds a co-taught lesson | Drag model blocks onto a 50-minute timeline and assign each teacher's role; get feedback on parity and MLL support |
| **7. The Research Library** | D1–D2 | Learns what the evidence says | **True/False quiz:** "Co-teaching alone reliably raises MLL scores" → False (explain why) |
| **8. The Pitfall Pit** | E1 | Avoids common traps | **Scenario cards:** "No planning time this week. What happens?" Choose the fix; wrong choices drop you into the "pit" with an explanation |
| **9. The Parity Mirror** | E2–E3 | Self-assesses a partnership | Interactive **parity checklist**; get a "partnership health" score and suggestions |
| **10. The Principal's Office** | E4, F | Advocates for system conditions | Role-play: ask the principal for planning time and caseload limits, using Roadmap Principle 3 and research evidence |
| **11. Final Challenge: "Co-Plan a Lesson"** | All | Full collaborative cycle | With a partner or NPC: given a unit (e.g., 6th grade social studies), **co-plan** one lesson (objectives, 2+ models, scaffolds by level), **co-assess** with a two-row rubric, and write a **reflection** |
| **12. Exit Door: Commitment** | – | One partnership action | Private + public: "With my co-teacher (or a colleague), I will…" |

**Room badge:** "Dynamic Duo" (or similar), earned after Stations 5 and 11.
**Carry-forward items:** the **Seven Models card**, a **co-planning protocol template**, and a **parity checklist**.
**Connections to earlier rooms:** objectives formula (Station 5), two-row rubric (Station 11), MLRs as station activities (Station 6), SPED + ELD collaboration from the IEP room.

## Quick-Reference Card (for participants)

1. **Two experts, one classroom:** content = destination, language = road.
2. **Co-teaching ≠ aide.** Rotate the lead, share the planning, share the grading.
3. **Plan → Teach → Assess → Reflect,** together, every week.
4. **Match the model to the goal.** Most models run 5–20 minutes, not the whole period.
5. **Write the language objective together** for every co-taught lesson.
6. **Flexible groups only.** Never the same MLLs in the "pre-teach" group every day.
7. **Co-teaching delivers integrated ELD. It doesn't replace designated ELD.**
8. **The research is promising but young.** Co-teaching works when language expertise drives instruction.
9. **Leaders make it possible:** protected planning time, reasonable caseloads, stable pairs.
