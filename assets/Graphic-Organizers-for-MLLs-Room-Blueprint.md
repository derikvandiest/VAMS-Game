# ELD PD Game: Room: Using Graphic Organizers to Support MLLs

**Topic:** Graphic organizers (GOs) as language and thinking scaffolds for Multilingual Learners, with ready-to-use examples
**Suggested campus level:** Early-to-middle room (6th grade). It's a very practical, high-leverage strategy that later rooms build on. It connects to:
- **Learning Objectives:** organizers make the language function visible
- **Algebra:** the Values/Units chart
- **MLLs with IEPs:** organizers are research-backed for students with learning disabilities
- **Co-Teaching:** organizers work well in stations
**Sources:** No director deck. Everything here comes from research (see the Sources section). Part A = what and why · Part B = what the research says · Part C = matching organizers to language functions · Part D = 12 example organizers (with language supports) · Part E = how to teach with organizers · Part F = California connections · Part G = game room blueprint

## PART A: What Are Graphic Organizers and Why Do They Help MLLs?

### A1. Definition
A **graphic organizer** is a visual display that shows **how ideas relate**: cause and effect, sequence, comparison, main idea and details, claim and evidence. It uses **shapes, lines, arrows, and space** instead of only paragraphs of text.

### A2. Why They Work for MLLs
| Benefit | How it helps an MLL |
|---|---|
| **Lowers the language load, not the thinking** | The *structure* of the ideas is visible, so students can show relationships with words, phrases, drawings, or L1 before writing full English sentences |
| **Makes text structure visible** | Students see that a text compares, explains a cause, or tells a sequence, and learn the **signal words** that go with each structure |
| **Bridges talk → writing** | Notes in the organizer become the raw material for speaking frames and then paragraphs |
| **Organizes vocabulary** | Words are grouped by meaning and relationship, not memorized in isolation |
| **Shows understanding without full English** | An Emerging student can show they understand cause and effect by placing pictures and key words, which is real content evidence (Grading for Equity room) |
| **Reduces cognitive load** | Working memory is freed from holding all the ideas, so it can focus on language and reasoning (especially helpful for MLLs with IEPs) |

> **Key idea:** a graphic organizer is a **thinking tool AND a language tool**. For MLLs, the best organizers have the **language built in**: signal words, word banks, and sentence frames.

## PART B: What the Research Says

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| **What Works Clearinghouse**, *Teaching Academic Content and Literacy to English Learners in Elementary and Middle School* (IES, 2014), **Rec. 2** (evidence: **strong**) | Teachers should **strategically use instructional tools, such as short videos, visuals, and graphic organizers, to anchor instruction** and help ELs make sense of content, as part of integrating oral and written language into content teaching |
| **National Academies** (2017), *Promoting the Educational Success of Children and Youth Learning English* | Making core content comprehensible with tools like visuals and graphic organizers is a practice linked to content learning for ELs |
| **Dexter & Hughes (2011)**, meta-analysis (*Learning Disability Quarterly*): 16 studies, 808 students, grades 4–12 | Graphic organizers improved **vocabulary, comprehension, and inferential knowledge** for students with learning disabilities, with **moderate to large** effects. Largest in **science** (ES ≈ 1.05), smallest in **math** (ES ≈ 0.59) |
| **Kim, Vaughn, Wanzek & Wei (2004)**, synthesis | Students with LD showed **greater reading comprehension gains** with graphic organizers than with other reading interventions |
| **Nesbit & Adesope (2006)**, meta-analysis of concept/knowledge maps (*Review of Educational Research*) | Concept maps improved **retention**, with **small to large** effects depending on how they were used. Constructing maps and studying maps both helped |
| **Studies with ELs** (smaller: theses, case studies, action research) | Organizers that **match the text's structure** improved ELs' reading comprehension; organizers helped ELs **plan and write** (including argumentative writing); a case study found organizers effective for an **EL with a learning disability** |
| **Jiang & Grabe**, "Graphic organizers in reading instruction" (*Reading in a Foreign Language*) | Recommends organizers for **recurring text structures**, but notes **inconsistent results** and calls for more research with second-language learners over longer periods |

> **Honest summary:** the evidence is **strongest for students with learning disabilities and for content comprehension**. For ELs, the strongest support is the WWC recommendation (organizers as part of integrated oral/written language instruction). EL-specific studies are promising but small. **Organizers work best when they are explicitly taught, matched to text structure, and paired with talk and writing**, not handed out as worksheets.

## PART C: Matching Organizers to Language Functions and Text Structures

Pick the organizer based on **what students are doing with language** (the language function from the Learning Objectives room).

| Language function / text structure | Organizer | Signal words | Sample frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Define / describe** | Frayer Model; Bubble Map / Web | *is, has, looks like, an example of* | "A ___ is a ___ that ___." |
| **Compare / contrast** | Venn Diagram; Double Bubble; T-Chart | *both, similarly, however, whereas, unlike* | "Both ___ and ___ ___, but ___." |
| **Sequence / explain a process** | Flow Chart; Timeline; First-Next-Then-Last | *first, next, then, after, finally* | "After ___, ___ happened." |
| **Cause and effect** | Multi-Flow Map; Cause-Effect Chain | *because, so, as a result, therefore, led to* | "___ caused ___ because ___." |
| **Main idea and details / classify** | Tree Map; Main Idea Table | *one, another, also, for example* | "One detail that supports ___ is ___." |
| **Problem / solution** | Problem-Solution Chart | *problem, solved by, one solution, as a result* | "The problem was ___. It was solved by ___." |
| **Argue / justify** | Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER); OREO / Opinion Map | *I claim, according to, this shows, therefore* | "I claim ___. The text states ___. This shows ___." |
| **Summarize / retell** | Story Map; Somebody-Wanted-But-So-Then | *in the beginning, wanted, but, so, in the end* | "___ wanted ___, but ___, so ___." |
| **Analyze parts and wholes** | Brace Map; labeled diagram | *is made of, part of, consists of* | "A ___ consists of ___, ___, and ___." |
| **Analogy / relationship** | Bridge Map | *is to, as* | "___ is to ___ as ___ is to ___." |
| **Take notes / connect ideas** | Cornell Notes; Concept Map | *relates to, leads to, is a type of* | "___ is related to ___ because ___." |
| **Math word problems** | Values/Units chart (Algebra room); Three Reads organizer | *per, each, total, less than* | "___ represents ___ per ___." |

> *Thinking Maps®* (Hyerle) is a well-known commercial set of eight maps (Circle, Bubble, Double Bubble, Tree, Brace, Flow, Multi-Flow, Bridge) that follows this same function-to-map idea. Several examples below use similar shapes.

## PART D: 12 Example Organizers (with Built-In Language Supports)

Each example includes the **organizer**, a **middle school content example**, and **language supports by proficiency level** (E = Emerging, X = Expanding, B = Bridging).

---

### D1. Frayer Model (Vocabulary) · *Define / Describe*
**Content:** 6th grade science, *photosynthesis*

```
┌──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┐
│ DEFINITION (own words)       │ CHARACTERISTICS              │
│ How plants make their own    │ • needs sunlight             │
│ food using sunlight, water,  │ • needs water + CO₂          │
│ and carbon dioxide           │ • makes sugar (glucose)      │
│                              │ • releases oxygen            │
├───────────── PHOTOSYNTHESIS / fotosíntesis ────────────────┤
│ EXAMPLES                     │ NON-EXAMPLES                 │
│ • a leaf in sunlight         │ • a person eating lunch      │
│ • algae in a pond            │ • a mushroom (fungus)        │
│ [drawing of leaf + sun]      │ • respiration                │
└──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```
**Language supports:**
- **E:** picture + **cognate** in the center (*fotosíntesis*); word bank (*sunlight, water, sugar, oxygen*); frame: "Photosynthesis needs ___."
- **X:** "Photosynthesis is the process in which ___ use ___ to make ___."
- **B:** "Unlike ___, photosynthesis ___, which is why ___."

### D2. Double Bubble / Venn Diagram · *Compare and Contrast*
**Content:** 7th grade social studies, *Aztec vs. Maya*

```
        AZTEC ONLY            BOTH               MAYA ONLY
   ┌────────────────┐  ┌──────────────┐  ┌────────────────────┐
   │ capital:       │  │ Mesoamerica  │  │ city-states         │
   │ Tenochtitlan   │  │ pyramids     │  │ (no single capital) │
   │ one empire     │  │ calendars    │  │ writing system with │
   │ chinampas      │  │ farming maize│  │ glyphs              │
   │ (floating      │  │ many gods    │  │ advanced astronomy  │
   │ farms)         │  │              │  │                     │
   └────────────────┘  └──────────────┘  └────────────────────┘
```
**Language supports:**
- **Signal word bank:** *both, also, similarly | however, but, whereas, unlike, on the other hand*
- **E:** "Both ___ and ___ had ___." / "The Aztec had ___. The Maya had ___."
- **X:** "Both civilizations ___, but the Aztec ___, while the Maya ___."
- **B:** "Although the Aztec and Maya both ___, they differed in ___; for example, ___."

### D3. Flow Chart / Sequence · *Explain a Process*
**Content:** 6th grade science, *the water cycle*

```
[ EVAPORATION ] ──► [ CONDENSATION ] ──► [ PRECIPITATION ] ──► [ COLLECTION ]
 sun heats water      vapor cools and       water falls as         water gathers in
 → water vapor        forms clouds          rain, snow, hail       oceans, lakes, ground
      ▲                                                                   │
      └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
**Language supports:**
- **Transition bank:** *first, next, then, after that, finally, the cycle repeats*
- **E:** draw each step; "First, ___. Next, ___."
- **X:** "After water evaporates, it ___ and forms ___."
- **B:** "As water vapor rises and cools, it condenses into clouds, which eventually ___."

### D4. Cause-and-Effect (Multi-Flow) · *Explain Causes and Effects*
**Content:** 8th grade history, *the American Revolution*

```
     CAUSES                                      EFFECTS
 ┌─────────────────────┐                  ┌──────────────────────────┐
 │ taxes without        │──┐          ┌──►│ Declaration of           │
 │ representation       │  │          │   │ Independence (1776)      │
 ├─────────────────────┤  │  ┌──────┐│   ├──────────────────────────┤
 │ Boston Massacre      │──┼─►│ AMER.│┼──►│ war with Britain          │
 ├─────────────────────┤  │  │ REV. ││   ├──────────────────────────┤
 │ Intolerable Acts     │──┘  └──────┘└──►│ new nation; Constitution │
 └─────────────────────┘                  └──────────────────────────┘
```
**Language supports:**
- **Signal words:** *because, due to, led to, as a result, therefore, consequently*
- **E:** "___ made colonists angry." / "The war happened because ___."
- **X:** "One cause of the Revolution was ___, which led to ___."
- **B:** "Due to ___, colonists ___; consequently, ___."

### D5. Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) · *Argue / Justify*
**Content:** 7th grade ELA or science, *"Should schools start later?"* or *"Which substance is an acid?"*

| **CLAIM** (answer the question) | **EVIDENCE** (data or quote from the text) | **REASONING** (why the evidence supports the claim) |
|---|---|---|
| Schools should start later. | "Teens who slept 8+ hours scored higher on tests" (Article, p. 2). | Sleep helps the brain remember, so later start times can help students learn better. |

**Language supports:**
- **E:** "I think ___." / "The text says ___." / "This shows ___."
- **X:** "I claim that ___. According to ___, ___. This evidence shows ___ because ___."
- **B:** "Based on ___, it is clear that ___. This supports my claim because ___. Some may argue ___; however, ___."
- **Citation verb bank** (from the director's deck, slide 13): *states, explains, shows, suggests, argues, reports*

---

### D6. Story Map / Somebody-Wanted-But-So-Then · *Summarize / Retell*
**Content:** any narrative (ELA)

| Somebody | Wanted | But | So | Then |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (character) | (goal) | (problem) | (action / solution) | (ending) |
| Esperanza | wanted a house of her own | her family could only afford a small, run-down house | she wrote and dreamed about her future | she promised to come back for others |

**Language supports:**
- **E:** sketch in each box + key words
- **X/B:** turn the row into one sentence: "**___ wanted ___, but ___, so ___. Then ___.**" That is a complete summary sentence!

---

### D7. Tree Map / Main Idea and Details · *Classify / Organize Information*
**Content:** 6th grade science, *types of rocks*

```
                       ROCKS
          ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
      IGNEOUS       SEDIMENTARY     METAMORPHIC
   • from cooled   • layers of     • changed by heat
     magma/lava      sediment        and pressure
   • e.g., granite  • e.g., sand-  • e.g., marble
     obsidian         stone, shale    slate
```
**Language supports:**
- **E:** "___ is a type of ___ rock."
- **X:** "Igneous rocks form when ___. One example is ___."
- **B:** "Unlike sedimentary rocks, which ___, metamorphic rocks ___."

---

### D8. Problem-Solution Chart · *Explain Problems and Solutions*
**Content:** 8th grade science/social studies, *water shortage in California*

| Problem | Causes | Possible solutions | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Not enough water in dry years | drought, population growth, farming needs | water recycling | reuses water | expensive to build |
| | | conservation rules | cheap, fast | people must change habits |

**Language supports:**
- **E:** "The problem is ___. One solution is ___."
- **X:** "One way to solve ___ is to ___. This would help because ___."
- **B:** "Although ___ would ___, it might also ___; therefore, the best solution is ___ because ___."

---

### D9. Cornell Notes (with a language column) · *Take Notes / Summarize*

| **Questions / key words** (left) | **Notes** (right) |
|---|---|
| What is a cell? *(célula)* | the smallest unit of life; all living things are made of cells |
| Plant vs. animal cell? | plant: cell wall, chloroplasts; animal: no cell wall |
| **Summary** (bottom): | *Cells are ___. Plant and animal cells are different because ___.* |

**MLL adaptations:**
- **Partially completed notes** (fill in the blanks): good for E and X levels and for students with IEPs
- Allow **L1 notes** in the left column; add cognates
- Provide the **summary frame** at the bottom

### D10. Values/Units Chart · *Math Word Problems* (from the Algebra Room / Stanford MLR6)

> "It costs $3 per person to go to the zoo. The family has a $5 coupon. There are p people."

| Value | Units / meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | $ per person |
| 5 | $ discount |
| p | number of people |
| C | total cost |

→ cost = ($ per person) × (number of people) − (discount) → **C = 3p − 5**

---

### D11. KWL+ / Anticipation Chart · *Activate Prior Knowledge*

| **K** – What I **K**now (any language) | **W** – What I **W**ant to know | **L** – What I **L**earned | **+** New words I learned |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volcanoes are hot. *Hay en México.* | Why do they erupt? | Pressure from magma pushes up. | *magma, erupt, crust, pressure* |

**Why it's MLL-friendly:** it honors **background knowledge from students' home countries and languages**, and the **+ column** builds a personal vocabulary log.

### D12. Paragraph / Essay Planner (Hamburger or Bridge to Writing) · *Write*

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ TOPIC SENTENCE:  ___ is important because ___.│   ← top bun
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ DETAIL 1:  First, ___.   (evidence: ___)      │
│ DETAIL 2:  Also, ___.    (evidence: ___)      │   ← fillings
│ DETAIL 3:  Finally, ___. (evidence: ___)      │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ CONCLUSION:  In conclusion, ___.              │   ← bottom bun
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
**Bridge to writing:** students fill the organizer with notes → **talk it through with a partner** (MLR1 Stronger & Clearer) → write the paragraph. This matches CA ELD standard **10b (Grade 7):** *"Write brief summaries of texts and experiences using complete sentences and key words (e.g., from notes or graphic organizers)."*

## PART E: How to Teach with Graphic Organizers

### E1. Gradual Release: Fade the Support
```
I DO            →   WE DO             →   YOU DO TOGETHER     →   YOU DO ALONE
Teacher models      Class completes        Partners complete       Student completes,
a completed         one together           a partially filled      then CREATES their
organizer +         (think-aloud)          organizer               own organizer
think-aloud
```
**Fading by proficiency level:**

| Emerging (substantial support) | Expanding (moderate) | Bridging (light) |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly completed organizer; pictures; word bank; L1 allowed; frames in every box | Partially completed; word bank; frames for the summary | Blank organizer or student-chosen organizer; optional frames |

### E2. The Organizer → Talk → Write Routine
1. **Read / listen / observe** (input)
2. **Organize** ideas in the organizer (individually or with a partner)
3. **Talk:** explain the organizer to a partner using the frames (oral rehearsal)
4. **Write:** turn the organizer into sentences or a paragraph
5. **Reflect:** "Which signal words did you use? How did the organizer help?"

### E3. Design Tips: "Language-Rich" Organizers
- [ ] **Match the organizer to the text structure / language function** (Part C)
- [ ] **Embed signal words and sentence frames** right on the organizer
- [ ] Add a **word bank** with visuals and **cognates**
- [ ] Leave room for **drawings and L1**
- [ ] Use **consistent shapes** across classes and grades so students recognize them (a schoolwide set)
- [ ] **Number the steps** and keep directions short
- [ ] Make it **accessible**: large font, clear boxes, digital versions with text-to-speech when needed (IEP)

### E4. Common Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Handing out organizers as **busy-work worksheets** | Use them for **thinking and talking**, connected to a task |
| **Too many different organizers** | Choose a **small, consistent set** used schoolwide |
| **Never fading** the organizer | Gradually release, until students **choose or create their own** |
| Organizer with **no language support** | Add signal words, frames, and word banks |
| Organizer is the **end product** | The organizer is a **bridge** to speaking and writing |
| **Grading** organizer neatness or spelling | Use it as **formative evidence of thinking** (Grading for Equity) |
| Wrong organizer for the text (e.g., Venn for a sequence) | Teach students to **identify the text structure first** |

### E5. Organizers in Co-Taught and IEP Settings
- **Co-teaching (Model #5 Pre-teach):** the ELD teacher previews the organizer and vocabulary with a small group before the lesson.
- **Co-teaching (Model #7 Stations):** one station = organizer completion with a teacher; another = partner talk using the organizer.
- **IEPs:** partially completed organizers, guided notes, and digital organizers are common **accommodations** (same standard, different access). Note them in the IEP when appropriate.

## PART F: California Connections

- **CA ELD Standards, Part I, Productive mode (strand 10b):** across grade levels, students "write… summaries of texts and experiences using complete sentences and key words (**e.g., from notes or graphic organizers**)," with expectations rising from *brief* (Emerging) to *increasingly concise* (Expanding) to *clear and coherent… complete and concise* (Bridging).
- **CA ELD Standards, planned scaffolding:** the standards list "using a range of information systems, **such as graphic organizers, diagrams, photographs, videos, or other multimedia**, to enhance access to content" as a planned scaffold, alongside language models like **sentence frames and vocabulary walls**.
- **CA ELD Proficiency Level Descriptors:** at the **Emerging** level, students can "**express ideas using visuals such as drawings, charts, or graphic organizers**." Organizers are an expected, standards-aligned way for newcomers to show learning.
- **CA ELA/ELD Framework:** classroom vignettes show teachers giving **Emerging** ELs graphic organizers (e.g., with the stages of a process) to support their explanations.
- **Part II, "Learning About How English Works":** organizers with **signal words** directly teach **Structuring Cohesive Texts** (text organization, cohesion) and **Connecting Ideas** (combining clauses with *because, although, as a result*).
- **CA EL Roadmap, Principle 2:** organizers are a scaffold for **meaningful access to grade-level content** without watering it down.

## PART G: Game Blueprint: The "Graphic Organizer" Classroom

**Room theme idea:** an "**Architect's Studio**" or "**Map Room**": organizers are blueprints and maps for thinking.

| Station (in-room object) | Content source | What the participant does | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Messy Desk** (hook) | A1–A2 | Experiences the need for organization | Read a dense paragraph in an unfamiliar language with **no organizer**, then the same information **in an organizer**. Reflect on the difference |
| **2. The Research Wall** | B | Learns the evidence | Flip cards: match each study to its finding; one "honest summary" card |
| **3. The Function-to-Map Matcher** | C | Picks the right organizer | **Drag-and-drop:** match 10 tasks ("compare two civilizations," "explain the water cycle") to the correct organizer |
| **4. The Signal Word Sorter** | C | Connects signal words to structures | Sort *because, whereas, finally, according to…* into structure bins |
| **5. The Organizer Gallery** | D1–D12 | Explores 12 example organizers | Walk-through gallery: click each frame to see a filled example and its language supports for E/X/B |
| **6. The Build-a-Frayer Station** | D1 | Completes a Frayer model | Complete a Frayer for a content word; choose a cognate and a non-example |
| **7. The Upgrade Lab** | E3 | Makes a plain organizer "language-rich" | Given a blank Venn, **add** a word bank, signal words, and E/X/B frames; checklist scoring |
| **8. The Fade Ladder** | E1 | Practices gradual release | Arrange 4 versions of the same organizer from most to least supported; assign each to a student profile (E/X/B/IEP) |
| **9. The Organizer → Talk → Write Studio** | E2 | Walks through the routine | Use an organizer → record or type a partner explanation with frames → write a paragraph |
| **10. The Pitfall Detector** | E4 | Spots misuse | Scenarios: "Students color the Venn diagram and turn it in for a grade." Identify the pitfall and the fix |
| **11. Final Challenge: "Design Your Own"** | All | Creates an organizer for their own lesson | Choose a lesson → identify the language function → pick or design the organizer → embed signal words, a word bank, and frames for 2 levels → plan the talk → write step. Self-check with the E3 checklist |
| **12. Exit Door: Commitment** | – | One organizer to use next week | Private + public: "Next week I'll use the ___ organizer for ___." |

**Room badge:** "Master Mapmaker" (or similar), earned after Stations 7 and 11.
**Carry-forward items:** a **Function-to-Organizer chart**, a **signal word bank**, and a **printable set of the 12 organizers** with frames.
**Connections to other rooms:** Learning Objectives (the function picks the organizer), Algebra (Values/Units), Grading for Equity (organizers as evidence of content thinking), MLLs with IEPs (accommodation), Co-Teaching (station use).

---

## Quick-Reference Card (for participants)

1. **Function first:** what are students doing with language? Pick the matching organizer.
2. **Build the language in:** signal words + word bank + sentence frames on every organizer.
3. **Organizer → Talk → Write.** The organizer is a bridge, not the final product.
4. **Model it first** (I do → we do → you do).
5. **Fade the support** by proficiency level, until students create their own.
6. **Use a small, consistent set** across classes.
7. **Welcome drawings, cognates, and L1** in the boxes.
8. **Don't grade neatness or spelling.** Use organizers as evidence of thinking.
